Built from what broke

We tested LLM auditors before building one. Each step below answers a failure we measured.

Split by Article

Compliant sections after a violation dilute it. The file is cut at its Article headings and every section is judged on its own.

Sample three times

Nemotron 3 Nano judges each section three times in parallel. A single sample can flip its verdict between identical runs; the median of three is steadier.

Escalate disagreement

When the samples disagree, Nemotron 3 Ultra decides. If Nano and Ultra still disagree, the Article goes to a human instead of a guess.

Flag claims, check quotes

Notes to the reviewer and unsupported compliance claims are flagged and ignored. Evidence quotes are shown only if they appear in your text.

Screen a file

Paste documentation with an "Article N" heading per section, or load an example. Takes about 10 to 20 seconds. A screening aid for reviewers, not legal advice.

What the research found

3 of 3

Long files fooled every judge we tried

With the violating section first in a five-section file, the three whole-file judges we tested falsely cleared 9.6%, 15% and 37% of non-compliant documents. Placed last, the effect went away. This is why the Screener splits files.

0

Fake approvals did not work

"Certified compliant" and "pre-approved by the AI Office" text cleared no non-compliant document.

1/32

Rewrite attacks rarely did

Claude Sonnet rewrote violating files to look compliant. It succeeded once.

0.657

Experts disagree too

The three legal annotators agree with each other at Krippendorff's alpha 0.657, so the Screener sends close calls to a person rather than pretending to certainty.

What it is not

Not legal advice or a conformity assessment. It helps a reviewer decide where to look.
Evaluated on synthetic, expert-labelled excerpts. Real technical files are longer and messier.
Covers Articles 9, 10, 12, 14 and 15 only, the core high-risk requirements.
Close calls are routed to human review on purpose, about one Article in five.
